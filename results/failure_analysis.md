# Categories (from reading 12 sampled failures, 4 per group)

| Category | Queries | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Vocabulary mismatch | 1, 1191 | Claim and abstract use different words; dense retrieval rescues these, BM25 cannot. |
| Spelling and tokenization | 1088, 1221 | Bcl2 vs BCL-2 and a typo in the query break exact matching. |
| Rare exact terms | 1024, 1041, 1179, 1180 | BM25 matches names like CTCF, H2A.Z and MDA5; dense retrieval blurs them into the general topic. |
| Negation | 1110 | Neither method handles "not predictive". |
| Right topic, wrong paper | 1049, 1199 | Both return papers on the same topic but not the one with the evidence. |
| Detail not in abstract | 1175 | The correct paper is a general review that does not state the detail. |

## Takeaways
- BM25 fails on 43 of 300 queries and dense on 62; only 33 fail on both, so the methods are complementary.
- BM25 rescues 29 dense failures, dense rescues 10 BM25 failures: exact terms matter more than meaning on scientific claims.
- Hybrid still fails on 44, so its gain comes mostly from better ranking, not from rescuing hard failures.
- Queries 1175, 1179 and 1180 share one correct document (a general RLR review) that dense retrieval misses every time.
- Limitation: categories come from 12 examples, not all 120 failing queries.
- Future work: a biomedical-aware tokenizer (gene names with hyphens and digits) and a cross-encoder reranker.
